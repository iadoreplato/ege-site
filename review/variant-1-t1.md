# variant-1-t1: Learning something new: six opinions

## Текст записи
**Announcer:** Speaker A

**Speaker A:** When I bought my guitar, I imagined I would play for hours every weekend. That lasted exactly two weeks. Then I tried something different: ten minutes every evening, even when I was tired or my fingertips hurt. A year on, I can play a dozen songs. My brother practises for three hours on Sundays and then not at all, and he is still stuck on the same chords. Small drops, it seems, fill the bucket better than the occasional wave.

**Announcer:** Speaker B

**Speaker B:** I would never have kept going with my Spanish if I had stayed at home with an app. The lessons were fine, but a little counter on the screen was the only thing pushing me forward. Then I joined an evening class with eight strangers. We laughed at one another's pronunciation, we helped with homework, and when I felt like skipping a lesson, I remembered that somebody would notice. Last week I ordered a whole dinner in Spanish. I couldn't have done it by myself.

**Announcer:** Speaker C

**Speaker C:** My first programme crashed eleven times in a row, and I nearly gave up. My teacher just smiled and said the crashes were my real lessons. He was right. Every error message told me exactly what I had misunderstood. Now, when something goes wrong, I'm almost glad, because I know I'm about to learn something I'll never forget. The people who never get anything wrong, he says, are usually the ones who never try anything difficult.

**Announcer:** Speaker D

**Speaker D:** My daughters laughed when I said I wanted to learn to swim at fifty-two. For years I had been too embarrassed even to mention it, because everybody else seemed to have learnt as a child. But the instructor at our local pool had pupils of every age, including a retired man of seventy-eight. I'm not fast, and I never will be, but last summer I swam across a lake. I only wish I hadn't waited so long.

**Announcer:** Speaker E

**Speaker E:** Photography started as something I did on holiday. Friends began asking me to take pictures at their weddings, and a local magazine noticed my website. Two years later I left my job in an office. I had never planned anything like it. If somebody had told me, when I bought my first camera, that it would become my profession, I would have laughed. Sometimes a pastime takes you somewhere you could never have mapped out in advance.

**Announcer:** Speaker F

**Speaker F:** I watched hundreds of cooking videos before I tried making bread myself. In every one of them it looked so easy. My first loaf was as hard as a brick. Then my neighbour, who bakes professionally, stood next to me for one afternoon and showed me how the dough should feel. I learnt more in those three hours than in all my evenings online. Films are great for ideas, but you need somebody to put their hands in the flour with you.

## Задание
- 1. Learning works best when you set yourself a clear goal.
- 2. Studying in a group can keep you motivated.
- 3. A hobby can unexpectedly turn into a profession.
- 4. Video lessons alone are not enough to master a practical skill.
- 5. Failure is a natural and useful part of learning.
- 6. Practising a little every day is more effective than rare long sessions.
- 7. Age should not stop anyone from learning something new.
