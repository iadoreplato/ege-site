# variant-2-t3: Young Entrepreneurs: a baker talks about his business
Задание 3, юнит 2, модуль variant

## Текст записи
**Presenter:** Today on Young Entrepreneurs I'm speaking to Oliver Grant, who opened his own bakery in Bristol at the age of twenty-three. Oliver, welcome.

**Oliver:** Thank you for having me.

**Presenter:** Most people your age are still deciding what to do with their lives. Why did you start baking?

**Oliver:** It began at university, where I was studying chemistry and planning to work in a laboratory. In my second year I started baking bread in my student flat, because the loaves in the shops were so bad. My flatmates began to pay me to make extra ones, and by the end of the year I was selling forty loaves a week from the kitchen window.

**Presenter:** That sounds like a hobby that got out of control.

**Oliver:** Exactly. But what finally convinced me was a summer I spent working in a small bakery in France. The owner got up at two in the morning every day, and he was the happiest man I'd ever met. I thought: if that's what a job looks like, I want one.

**Presenter:** Did your family support the idea?

**Oliver:** My parents were doubtful, to put it politely. They'd paid for my degree, after all. My grandmother was the only one who took it seriously, and she lent me the money for my first oven, which was second-hand and very temperamental.

**Presenter:** What was the hardest part of the first year?

**Oliver:** The hours, honestly. A baker's day starts in the middle of the night, so my social life more or less disappeared. But I'd expected that. What I hadn't expected was the amount of official paperwork: taxes, insurance, health inspections. I spent more time with forms than with dough.

**Presenter:** Your bakery is known for using local flour. Why is that important?

**Oliver:** Partly for flavour, because flour from a nearby mill tastes different from the industrial kind, and partly because I like knowing the farmers. But it isn't cheap, and some customers complain about the price. I tell them to taste the bread and decide for themselves.

**Presenter:** Have you ever had a disaster in the bakery?

**Oliver:** Plenty. The worst was last winter, when I mixed up salt and sugar in a batch of two hundred rolls. I only noticed when the first customer came back, looking rather pale. I gave everybody their money back and a free loaf, and strangely, that made them trust me more.

**Presenter:** What would you say to young people with an idea for a business?

**Oliver:** Test it before you give anything up. Sell something at a weekend market, ask people what they think, and don't be afraid of criticism. And be prepared to work harder than you've ever worked, but on something that belongs to you.

**Presenter:** Oliver, thank you very much.

**Oliver:** Thank you.
