# variant-2-t1: Lessons from travelling: six opinions

## Текст записи
**Announcer:** Speaker A

**Speaker A:** Before going to Portugal I spent a month learning phrases from a phone app, and I felt silly saying them aloud on the first morning. The baker, however, smiled so widely that she gave me a free pastry. Nobody expects a visitor to speak perfectly. But a 'good morning' and a 'thank you' in their own language told people that I respected their country, and doors opened everywhere, from taxis to family kitchens.

**Announcer:** Speaker B

**Speaker B:** On my first trip abroad I packed three pairs of boots, two coats and a hairdryer. The airline lost my suitcase for four days, and I discovered that the small bag I had kept with me contained everything I really needed. Since then I carry one backpack, whatever the destination. I move through stations faster, nobody waits for me at baggage reclaim, and I'm never tempted to buy souvenirs, because I simply have no room.

**Announcer:** Speaker C

**Speaker C:** We were heading for a famous castle when our train broke down in the middle of nowhere. The driver suggested a village two kilometres away, and we walked there rather reluctantly. It turned out to be market day: there was music, grandmothers selling honey, and a man who insisted on showing us his collection of old bicycles. We never reached the castle. If you asked me now, I would say that afternoon was the best of the whole holiday.

**Announcer:** Speaker D

**Speaker D:** Guidebooks tell you where to eat, but they can't tell you what people are really worried about or what makes them laugh. In Georgia I stayed with a family whose daughter was my age, and over breakfast she explained local traditions, the school system and even the jokes that everybody tells. I learnt more in a week at that table than in a month of museum visits. Whenever I can, I choose to stay with the people who actually live there.

**Announcer:** Speaker E

**Speaker E:** Many of my friends think that a good trip means a luxury hotel and a rented car. I went around Scotland last summer with a tent, a bicycle and about two hundred pounds. I washed in rivers, cooked on a small stove and met more interesting people on campsites than I would have in any resort. It was tough sometimes, particularly in the rain, but I came home with stories I still tell every week.

**Announcer:** Speaker F

**Speaker F:** Last year six of us travelled to Italy, and by the third day we were arguing about everything: Francesca wanted museums, Tom wanted the beach, and I just wanted lunch. We finally agreed to take turns choosing the day's programme, and, strangely, I enjoyed the museums more than I had expected. Going with others means you won't always get your way, but you also see things you would never have chosen for yourself.

## Задание
- 1. An inexpensive journey can be just as satisfying as a costly one.
- 2. An unexpected change of plan can turn into the highlight of a journey.
- 3. Travelling in a group means being ready to give way to others.
- 4. It is worth learning some phrases of the local language.
- 5. Contact with local residents shows you what a place is really like.
- 6. Photographs are a poor substitute for real memories.
- 7. Taking only a small amount of luggage makes travelling easier.
