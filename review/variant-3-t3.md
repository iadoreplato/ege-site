# variant-3-t3: Behind the Lens: a wildlife photographer
Задание 3, юнит 3, модуль variant

## Текст записи
**Presenter:** This week on Behind the Lens my guest is Anna Kowalski, a wildlife photographer whose pictures have appeared in magazines all over Europe. Anna, welcome.

**Anna:** Thank you, it's a pleasure.

**Presenter:** You started when you were fourteen. What did you photograph first?

**Anna:** Our neighbour's cat, if you can believe it. My grandfather gave me an old camera, and for months I photographed anything that moved in the garden: sparrows, hedgehogs, a very unfriendly squirrel. Most of the pictures were awful, but one of the hedgehogs won a school competition, and that was the moment I thought it might be more than a hobby.

**Presenter:** Wildlife photography sounds romantic. What is the reality?

**Anna:** Mostly waiting. I once spent nine days in a tiny shelter in the Carpathians hoping to photograph a lynx, and I never saw one. I came home with pictures of fog. People imagine adventure, but the work is largely patience, cold feet and cold sandwiches.

**Presenter:** Has it ever been dangerous?

**Anna:** Less than you'd think. The worst moment was in Norway, when I misjudged the ice on a frozen lake and fell through up to my waist. Fortunately my guide was close. Since then I always travel with a partner, whatever the location.

**Presenter:** Many photographers now use drones. What do you think of that?

**Anna:** I have mixed feelings. Drones allow pictures that were impossible before, such as a bird's-eye view of a migrating herd. But they can frighten animals, especially birds with nests, so I only use one where it's permitted and never near breeding colonies. A good picture is never worth disturbing a living creature.

**Presenter:** What about editing? Is it acceptable to improve a photograph with software?

**Anna:** Small corrections, yes: brightness, cropping, removing dust. What's unacceptable is adding things that weren't there. A few years ago a famous competition withdrew an award because the winner had moved an animal into the picture. It destroyed his reputation overnight, and rightly so.

**Presenter:** What equipment would you recommend for a beginner?

**Anna:** Honestly, nothing expensive. A modern phone has an excellent camera, and what matters in the beginning is learning to look: how light falls, how an animal moves, when to press the button. I know professionals who still do their best work with one cheap lens.

**Presenter:** And finally, what are you working on now?

**Anna:** A project about urban wildlife: foxes in car parks, falcons on skyscrapers. It's important to show that nature isn't something far away; it's all around us, if we pay attention.

**Presenter:** Anna Kowalski, thank you very much.

**Anna:** Thank you.
